# Localization notes

Written 2026-08-22, after correcting coin-face terminology in 11 locales. It records what was
verified, what was assumed, and what is still wrong, so the next person does not have to
re-derive it. The app ships English plus 12 translations; none were done by a human translator.

## Provenance

Every non-English string originated from a machine translation service. That is worth stating
plainly because it sets the quality bar: the existing text is *not* a trustworthy reference for
new work. Users have reported errors more than once.

The characteristic failure is a word translated in isolation, without the coin-toss context:

- **Anatomical calques.** "Heads"/"tails" rendered as body parts. Fixed; see below.
- **Dangling function words.** The bare word "About" became `Um` (de), `Di` (it), `O` (pl),
  `에 대한` (ko) — prepositions and postpositions with nothing attached. Fixed; see below.
- **Register drift.** Descriptions of what a setting does became instructions to the user
  ("Render *you* a spinning coin"), and second-person forms are mixed within a single locale.
  Partly fixed; see below.

## Coin faces

The two most visible strings in the app. `heads` and `tails` render at 72sp alone on the main
screen, label the Diagnostics stats rows, and supply the defaults for custom labels.

### The rule

**Portrait or national-arms side = heads. Value or inscription side = tails.** This is the anchor
for every locale, and it matches how the `*_heads` and `*_tails` drawables are cut. Apply it to any
new locale rather than translating the English words.

### The trap: two idioms run backwards

French and Turkish both name the tails side *first* in their set phrase:

- "**pile** ou **face**" — but `face` is the effigy side, so **face is heads**.
- "**yazı** **tura**" — but `tura` (from the Ottoman *tuğra*, the sultan's monogram) is the
  pictured side, so **tura is heads**.

Anyone reading the idiom left-to-right and mapping it onto "heads or tails" gets both wrong. This
is almost certainly how they were scrambled originally, and it is a mistake that survives review
because both words are real and both look plausible. Verify against the physical coin, not the
phrase.

## Regional variants

`values-es-rMX` exists because Mexico says "águila o sol", not "cara o cruz" — one of the few
splits that replaces *both* words rather than one.

It contains **four strings, not a full translation**. Android resolves resources per string, so
anything absent falls through to `values-es`. Future Spanish edits reach Mexico automatically
instead of needing to be applied twice. Lint accepts this and does not demand the other 62.

Use the same approach for any future regional variant. Do not clone a full locale file.

## Assumptions made

Recorded so they can be challenged:

1. **Formal/neutral register throughout.** No locale uses familiar second-person forms
   consistently, so new strings were written neutral. The existing files are inconsistent about
   this — Spanish has both `Toca` (tú) and `Toque/agite` (usted).
2. **All caps for coin faces in Latin scripts**, matching the English. Scripts without case are
   left as-is. `ÁGUILA` keeps its accent under capitalization, which RAE requires.
3. **Section headers are bare nouns** ("Coin", "Flip", "Advanced") rather than "X Settings",
   since the app bar already says Settings.
4. **No runtime case conversion anywhere in the app**, so Turkish dotless-i is not a hazard. If
   you ever add `.uppercase()`, pass a locale explicitly — `YAZI`/`yazı` is exactly the pair that
   breaks under a default-locale uppercase.
5. **`android.R.string.*` and framework strings are safe to reuse** for generic labels (`ok`,
   `cancel`, `search_go`); they come pre-localized. **AppCompat `abc_*` resources are not** — they
   are private implementation detail and were deliberately replaced with a project-owned
   `navigate_back`.

## Not confident

Ranked by how much a native check would help.

- **Hindi register (medium confidence).** The `चित`/`पट` mapping is solid, but whether Hindi users
  *want* Devanagari here is a real question: four of five Hindi coin apps on Play use transliterated
  `हेड्स`/`टेल्स`. `चित`/`पट` was chosen because the previous `सिर`/`पूंछ` was wrong under any
  reading and `चित`/`पट` is at worst slightly formal. `हेड्स`/`टेल्स` is the fallback if anyone
  complains. Note also a spelling split: everyday usage prefers `चित`/`पट`, hi.wikipedia uses
  `चित्त`/`पट्ट`.
- **Thai `หัว` standing alone (high confidence, minor caveat).** `ก้อย` is unambiguous next to a
  coin. `หัว` alone is polysemous (head / leader / beginning), but it is what Thai coin apps
  already display and the coin image disambiguates.
- **Korean `앞면`/`뒷면` vs `동전 앞면`.** The bare forms are correct but generic ("front/back
  face"). A Korean speaker may prefer something more coin-specific.
- **Every non-coin string added in 2026-08.** `clear`, `navigate_back`, the five section headers,
  the coin-group labels, `settings_item_coin_empty`, `settings_item_custom_text_title`,
  `settings_item_reset_stats_summary`, `diagnostics_iterations_range`. These are short and common,
  and they are all my own machine translation. Nobody has reviewed them.

## Full consistency pass

Every locale was then read end to end against English, applying one standard throughout:

1. **Terminology.** One English concept, one target word. The flip verb, the shake verb, "coin",
   "statistics" and "reset" were each split across two or more words in most locales.
2. **Register.** Settings summaries describe what a setting does, so they take an infinitive or
   verbal noun. Strings that genuinely instruct the user — the main-screen prompt, "Shake device
   to flip coin" — stay imperative, consistently formal within a locale.
3. **Self-reference.** `app_name` and `about_fragment_title` must name the same thing. Several
   locales named the app one way in the launcher and another in its own About screen; three still
   referenced the English "Coin Flip".
4. **Part of speech.** Toggle titles are nouns where English uses a noun. Scale labels
   (`force_low/medium/high`) are bare, not adjectival forms needing a following noun.
5. **Capitalization follows the target language, not English.** Turkish reserves Title Case for
   proper nouns and titles of works, so its labels are sentence case throughout; 15 strings were
   converted. Proper nouns (Google Play, Kanada, Turbo) and the deliberately upper-case display
   strings (`heads`, `tails`, `time`, `total`) keep their capitals. Note that İ lowercases to a
   dotted i, not a dotless ı.

Roughly 140 strings changed. The defects worth knowing about, because they show what machine
translation does when nobody reads the output:

- **`pl/seconds` was `%1stowary drugiej jakości`** — "seconds" read as factory seconds, i.e.
  defective goods. The Diagnostics screen rendered "7,261towary drugiej jakości". It had also
  eaten the space after the placeholder.
- **`tr/settings_item_dynamic_colors_summary` was `…kullanma`** — the *negative* imperative. The
  summary told users not to use wallpaper colors.
- **`pl/settings_item_force_title` was `Wstrząsanie wrażliwością`** — "shaking with sensitivity",
  word salad. `th` had the same defect: `เขย่าความไว`.
- **`ko/settings_item_animation_title` was `생기`** ("vitality") and **`zh` was `动画片`**
  ("animated film"). Neither is a UI animation.
- **`hi/diagnostics_fragment_title` contained two U+200B zero-width spaces**, and
  **`th/donate_text` had lost its heart emoji**, leaving a trailing space.
- **`ja` and `zh` `turbo_mode` both carried a stray `?`** after the emoji.
- **"Running total" became a running *process*** in `pt` (`em execução`), `tr` (`çalışan`),
  `zh` (`运行`), `hi` (`चालू`) and `it` (`totale parziale`, a subtotal).
- **Numeral agreement**: `tr`, `hi` and `pl` pluralized the noun after a number where their
  grammar does not. Turkish showed "1.000.000 yinelemeler".

A mechanical scan now guards the file set for invisible characters, stray whitespace, doubled
spaces, and placeholder drift against English. It reports clean.

## Known remaining issues

- **Polish and Turkish keep bare imperatives** in a few summaries. That is normal register for
  settings text in both languages, unlike the German and Spanish polite imperatives that were
  changed.
- **`hi` and `th` had the least scrutiny.** Both were reviewed against the same checklist and the
  clear defects are fixed, but my ability to catch a subtly unnatural phrasing is weakest there.
- **The English source says "Render spinning coin".** Locales that translated *render* literally
  were moved to "show"/"display", which now diverges slightly from the source. Fixing that
  properly starts with the English string.

## Mechanics

- **`MissingTranslation` is an error, not a warning.** `./gradlew :coinflip:check` fails if any
  translatable string is absent from any locale. Adding an English string means adding 12
  translations in the same commit. Regional variants are exempt via language fallback.
- **`translatable="false"`** marks the three strings that must not be translated:
  `about_mint_text`, `copyright_text`, `empty`.
- **`empty` is a self-closing tag** with no value. `Coin.Value.UNKNOWN` resolves to it, which is
  why `ResultText` in `MainScreen.kt` pins an explicit `lineHeight` — an empty string measures
  shorter than a word and everything below it would shift on the first flip.
- **Files are sorted by key.** Keep them that way; it makes cross-locale diffs readable.
- **Key prefixes name the feature**, not the screen that happened to use them first. The
  `diagnostics_iterations_*` pair was renamed out of `settings_item_*` when the control moved.

## Custom labels interact with all of this

`settings_item_custom_text_title` lets a user override `heads`/`tails`. An empty field stores
`null` and falls back to the localized default, shown as the field's placeholder.

This matters for translation work: it used to store the localized default *as a literal* when the
field was blank, which froze the label to whatever language was active and left no way back.
Anyone who had opened that dialog would never have seen a corrected coin face. If you change that
behaviour, preserve the null path.

## Sources worth reusing

Authorities beat dictionaries for this domain; dictionaries are what produced the anatomical
calques in the first place.

- Thai: [Office of the Royal Society](http://legacy.orst.go.th/) — defines หัว and ก้อย by which
  face they name
- Turkish: TDK (Türk Dil Kurumu) — *tura* = "metal paranın resimli yüzü"
- Hindi: [hi.wikipedia प्रायिकता सिद्धांत](https://hi.wikipedia.org/wiki/प्रायिकता_सिद्धांत) —
  glosses both terms by physical face; NCERT maths textbooks use them throughout
- Any locale: the national mint or central bank describing the obverse and reverse of current
  coinage, plus coin-flip apps and websites already published in that language
